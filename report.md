## Task 1: Digital Literacy Awareness Infographic

**Tool Used:** Canva

Creating this infographic using Canva was both educational and creatively fulfilling. I found that Canva's template library made it surprisingly easy to produce a professional-looking design even without prior graphic design experience. The drag-and-drop interface allowed me to focus on content organization rather than technical design challenges.

The most interesting aspect was realizing how much information could be condensed into a single visual page. I had to carefully select the most important points for each topic, which required critical thinking about what students truly need to know. This process helped me understand digital literacy more deeply myself.

One difficulty I encountered was balancing text density with visual appeal. Initially, I included too much text, making the infographic look cluttered. I learned that effective visual communication often means saying more with less. I had to iterate several times to find the right balance between informative and overwhelming.

Overall, this task taught me that good design is as much about editing and restraint as it is about creativity.

## Task 2: Build Your Student Digital Portfolio

**Platforms:** GitHub, LinkedIn, Kaggle

Setting up these three digital platforms was an eye-opening experience that made me realize how important online presence is for modern students. I chose GitHub, LinkedIn, and Kaggle because together they cover the three key aspects of a student's digital identity: technical work (GitHub), professional networking (LinkedIn), and domain-specific learning (Kaggle).

GitHub was the most straightforward to set up since I already had some familiarity with it from school projects. Creating the profile README felt like writing a mini-resume, which made me think seriously about how I want to present myself. LinkedIn required more thought because it's where I'll be visible to recruiters and professionals. I was careful to keep the tone professional while still being authentic. Kaggle excited me the most because I'm interested in data science, and seeing the community there motivated me to start learning seriously.

Over the next four years, I plan to use GitHub as my primary code portfolio, LinkedIn for networking and opportunity discovery, and Kaggle for skill development in data science. By graduation, I want all three profiles to tell a cohesive story of my growth as a professional.

## Task 3: Explore Coding & Collaboration Platforms

**Platforms:** HackerRank, Google Forms

Working with these two platforms showed me how technology can support both individual skill development and collaborative work. HackerRank's instant feedback mechanism was particularly valuable - I could immediately see which test cases failed and why. This kind of rapid iteration is essential for learning programming effectively. The gamification elements like points and badges also made the experience engaging rather than tedious.

Google Forms, on the other hand, demonstrated the power of cloud collaboration. Being able to create a form that automatically collects and organizes responses in a spreadsheet is incredibly useful for academic and extracurricular activities. I can imagine using this for class feedback surveys, event registrations, or even data collection for projects. The fact that multiple people can edit the form simultaneously and that everything is saved automatically makes it far superior to traditional offline tools.

Academically, these tools will help me in several ways. HackerRank will be my go-to platform for practicing coding problems, especially when preparing for technical interviews. Google Forms and Sheets will be invaluable for group projects, allowing seamless collaboration with teammates.

## Task 4: Professional Email & Etiquette Guide

In 2023, a university student in Karnataka sent an angry email to their professor after receiving a lower grade. The email, sent late at night without proofreading, contained informal language, accusatory statements, and threats. The professor forwarded it to the department head, and the student was called for a disciplinary meeting. The grade was not changed, and the student developed a reputation that affected future recommendation letters.

This could have been avoided by:
1. Waiting 24 hours before sending
2. Requesting a meeting instead of emailing
3. Using proper email format with respectful tone
4. Focusing on learning rather than demanding
5. Proofreading before sending

This scenario taught me that digital communication is permanent and can have lasting consequences. A single poorly written email can damage relationships, reputations, and opportunities. Professional email etiquette isn't about being formal for no reason - it's about showing respect and achieving goals effectively.

## Task 5: Cybercrime Awareness Case Study

**Topic:** Phishing Attacks

What surprised me most while researching phishing attacks was how sophisticated these scams have become. I always assumed phishing emails would be obvious - full of spelling errors and from suspicious addresses. However, modern phishing can be nearly indistinguishable from legitimate communications.

The scale was also shocking - over 2,500 complaints about a single scholarship scam with ₹15+ crores in losses. I was also startled to learn that many victims don't report cybercrimes due to embarrassment. The existence of the 1930 helpline was news to me - I had no idea there was a dedicated 24x7 cybercrime helpline in India.

Another eye-opening discovery was that receiving money can also be a scam - sharing a QR code can drain your account if you enter your UPI PIN.

As a result, I will immediately enable Two-Factor Authentication on all my accounts and start verifying links before clicking. These simple habits could prevent becoming a victim.
